# E4b: a decoder-only model given an encoder, Gemma 3 1B with the T5Gemma 2 1b-1b encoder attached

Written before the run, 2026-09-21. My starting intuition: take a decoder-only model, whose self-attention still sees every token, and attach a new encoder through cross-attention, to see whether the decoder gains anything from having an encoder in front of it. Concretely, Gemma 3 1B paired with the encoder half of T5Gemma 2 1b-1b. Named E4b rather than E6 because it is closer to E4.

## Hypothesis

E4 (the native hybrid) asks whether a cross-attention decoder gains from also reading the raw prompt; its decoder was pretrained to depend on the encoder, so a null result there says the decoder already had what it needed. This arm asks the converse: does a decoder that already reads the whole prompt through causal self-attention gain anything from a bidirectional encoder's view of the same prompt? Same idea as BERT-fused NMT and the CALM composition, on our task, with an exact control.

## Prediction

- The control is E1 on Gemma 3 1B (`e1-gemma3-1b-pt`, tt-decoder/om6sbier): the same decoder, the same input, the same recipe, the same loss, and at step 0 the same model (the cross-attention output projections start at zero). Benchmark: macro-F1 0.651 clean / 0.640 messy; unseen-question macro-F1 0.496 / 0.493; evidence F1 0.530 / 0.500.
- If the encoder's view adds information the decoder cannot recover from the raw tokens: E4b above the control on evidence F1 on both variants and on clean unseen-question macro-F1, by more than 0.02.
- If E4b lands between the control and the 3.9B Gemma 3 decoder (E1 `e1-gemma3-4b-pt`), the encoder helps but no more than a larger decoder does; if above the 4B on those slices, the effect is architectural, not capacity.
- **The hypothesis "an encoder in front helps a decoder that sees everything" is disconfirmed if** E4b is within 0.02 of the control on those slices *and* the cross-attention share of the residual stream (`cross_ratio_mean`, logged at every validation) has grown well above zero, i.e. the decoder read the encoder and gained nothing. If the share stays near zero the run says "not exploited within 2.8k steps", which is reported as inconclusive, not as a negative.
- Confound, accepted: E4b has about twice the parameters of the control (a second 1B tower plus the cross-attention). The 4B decoder is the capacity reference.

## Setup

- Model (`src/train/hybrid.py`, `model.arch: hybrid`): decoder `google/gemma-3-1b-pt` fed start token, prompt, answer, causal self-attention over all of it (E1's input exactly; `collate_encdec(dec_prompt=True, dec_start=False)`); encoder the text encoder of `google/t5gemma-2-1b-1b` (its image tower, projector and decoder dropped) over the prompt; a cross-attention sublayer in every one of the 26 decoder layers between self-attention and the MLP in Gemma's pre/post-norm arrangement, queries, keys, values, q/k norms and output projection copied from that layer's self-attention, the sublayer's post-norm weight at −1 so its output (1 + w)·rms(x) is exactly zero, no rotary embedding. The gate is the norm weight, not a zero output projection: under Gemma's post-norm a zero projection is zero only at step 0 (the norm rescales whatever it produces to unit size after the first update; the first smoke went 1.61 → 8.4 in three steps with gradient norm 9,400 from the norm's slope at zero). Fixed 2026-09-23. The two checkpoints tokenise identically (checked on real prompts; both Gemma tokenisers, vocabulary 262,144); the decoder's tokenizer serves both towers.
- Training: E1's recipe (lr 1e-5, cosine, 50 warm-up steps, AdamW 8-bit with fp32 masters, weight decay 0.1, one epoch, 32 sequences per step), every parameter trained, DDP, 16k cap and 16k micro-batch as the T5Gemma 2 pair. The gate trains at 100× the base rate and the copied cross-attention projections at 10× (`optim.lr_mult: {post_cross_layernorm: 100, cross: 10}`): Adam moves a weight by at most the learning rate per step, so at 10× the gate could open to 0.17 at most over 2.8k steps; at 100× it can open fully if the encoder is useful. Decoder under FlashAttention 2; the encoder under SDPA (T5Gemma 2 has no flash path).
- Diagnostics: at every validation, the mean over layers of ||cross-attention output|| / ||residual stream|| and the largest layer's (`cross_ratio_mean`, `cross_ratio_max`, wandb), to tell "did not use the encoder" from "used it and gained nothing".
- Cross-attention kernel: in training the cross-attention runs through FlashAttention's variable-length kernel over the real tokens of both sides (O(tokens) memory); the first launch died in the backward on a 16k example because SDPA with a key mask materialises the 16k × 16k matrix per layer (1.8 GiB) on a card already at 29 GiB. Probe after the change, one card, full step with masters and optimizer on the longest training example (16,251 tokens): 23.4 GiB. Generation keeps SDPA with cached encoder keys.
- Generation: `generate.py --backend hybrid`: encoder pass with the per-layer keys and values cached, decoder prefilled with the prompt (left-padded, explicit positions from 0) and greedy decoding of the label with a growing cache.
- Tests: the hybrid equals the plain Gemma 3 forward at initialisation; the cross path is live once its projection is non-zero; padded encoder keys are never attended; checkpointed training matches eval; cached decoding matches the full forward; learning-rate multipliers land on the named parameters.
- Smoke (2026-09-23, one process, 8k micro-batch, per-step log): loss 1.61 / 1.61 / 1.51 / 1.50 over four steps, grad norm 19–22, val 1.707 at step 4 (the control's step-0 val: 1.732); 23.3 GiB; 5.3k tokens/s on one card. 16k micro-batches ran out of memory at the checkpoint run (29 GiB at three steps), hence 8k. Chain (2026-09-23, both cards): three steps at 9.6k tokens/s, 27.2 GiB, val 1.725 at step 3; generation from the untrained hybrid through the `hybrid` backend regurgitates the prompt as an untrained decoder should; checkpoint run: step-0 val 1.7316, identical to the control's step-0 val to four decimals; resume from step-2 to step-4 clean (val 1.710) once checkpoint states load to CPU first (loading them onto the card alongside the live masters ran out of memory at 2.08B parameters); E4c two frozen-decoder steps: loss 1.58, 16.1 GiB, grad norm 0.13 (only the gates carry gradient while closed).
- wandb: tt-encdec, run id in `checkpoints/e4b-gemma3-1b-t5gemma2-enc/wandb_id`. Queue 17 started 2026-09-23 12:17; died in the backward at the first long example (see the kernel note); relaunched 12:22.

## Result

Training (tt-encdec, 2026-09-23 12:22 → 19:44, 7.37 h, about 10k tokens/s over the pair, 27 GiB): validation loss identical to the control at every reading, 1.7316 (0), 0.9219 / 0.9214 (200), 0.8373 / 0.8372 (400), 0.7949 / 0.7967 (600), 0.7649 / 0.7642 (800), 0.7401 / 0.7421 (1000), 0.7179 / 0.7173 (1200), 0.7053 / 0.7051 (1400), 0.6854 / 0.6847 (1600), 0.6717 / 0.6719 (1800), 0.6590 / 0.6586 (2000), 0.6489 / 0.6488 (2200), 0.6430 / 0.6422 (2400), 0.6370 / 0.6371 (2600), 0.6335 / 0.6336 (2800), 0.6320 / 0.6320 (2858), E4b / control. The gate never opened: |1 + w| 0.008 at step 200, 0.011 at step 400, 0.009 mean and 0.014 max in the final model; the cross-attention's share of the residual stream stayed at 0.0002–0.0003 mean, 0.0017–0.0027 max, at every validation. The gradient on the gate was noise: at 100× the base rate a consistent push would have opened it fully within a few hundred steps.

Benchmark (38,220 outputs over 20,700 pairs; generation 19:44 → 02:51 through the hybrid backend, with a restart after the SDPA cross-attention ran out of memory on the longest prompts), E4b against its control:

| | macro-F1 clean / messy | unseen-question macro-F1 clean / messy | evidence F1 clean / messy | format valid clean / messy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E4b (Gemma 3 1B + T5Gemma 2 encoder, gated cross-attention) | 0.641 / 0.630 | 0.477 / 0.487 | 0.523 / 0.491 | 0.944 / 0.930 |
| Gemma 3 1B (E1, the control) | 0.651 / 0.640 | 0.496 / 0.493 | 0.530 / 0.500 | 0.955 / 0.946 |

E4b sits 0.007–0.019 below the control on every headline number and on most slices (no slice above it by more than 0.014); val split clean macro-F1 0.664 vs 0.687, evidence 0.559 vs 0.578.

## Verdict

Not exploited, as pre-registered: the gate stayed at about 1% for the whole run and the validation loss was the control's to three decimals, so the run is the E1 decoder with an unused encoder attached. The benchmark reads 0.01 below the control rather than equal. Two contributions cannot be separated here: the 1%-open gate leaks a small untrained signal into every layer at generation time, and the control was generated through vLLM while E4b went through the Hugging Face loop (different kernels, same greedy decoding); generating the control through the same loop would settle the split and is cheap. Either way the difference is within what a seed replicate would show, and the substantive result is the gate: a decoder that already reads the whole prompt has no local incentive to open a path to an encoder within a 2.8k-step task fine-tune, because at the E1 solution the gradient on the gate is noise, and while the gate is closed the path cannot learn to become useful. The question "would an encoder help if it were read" is left to E4c (the path forced by freezing the decoder) and to the parked adaptation phase (the path taught before the task).
